# Art guide: Northern Song China for *Outlaws of the Marsh*

These notes are for whoever, or whatever, draws the replacement art: tiles, character sprites and portraits. The story
takes place under Emperor Huizong of the Northern Song, around 1100–1125, with a prologue under Renzong (1022–1063).
Everything should look like **eleventh- and early twelfth-century China**: not Ming or Qing, and not Japan or Korea.

## The best visual sources

- **Zhang Zeduan, *Along the River During the Qingming Festival*** (清明上河圖, early 12th century). This painted scroll of
  Kaifeng, the game's Eastern Capital, shows its streets, shops, bridges, boats and crowds. It is the most useful
  single reference: if something appears in it, it belongs in the game.
- ***Yingzao Fashi*** (營造法式, 1103). The Song government's building manual: roof forms, brackets, colour schemes,
  window and door patterns.
- **Surviving Song buildings.** The Hall of the Holy Mother at Jinci in Taiyuan; the Moni Hall at Longxing Temple in
  Zhengding; the Iron Pagoda of Kaifeng.
- **Song figure painting**, for clothing: Li Gonglin's ink figures; *The Eighteen Scholars*; *Palace Ladies Bathing
  Children*. The Southern Tang *Night Revels of Han Xizai* is a century earlier but close.

## What to avoid

| Avoid | Why | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| Shaved forehead and long braid (the queue) | Qing, 17th century onward | Hair in a topknot under a cap or cloth wrap |
| Mandarin collars, horse-hoof cuffs, embroidered rank squares | Qing official dress | Round-collared robe with a belt |
| Steep thin roofs, sliding paper walls, torii, katana | Japanese | Heavy tiled roofs, swinging double doors, straight Chinese swords (*jian*) and sabres (*dao*) |
| Wildly curled eaves and very ornate glazed roofs everywhere | Late southern Qing style | Song eaves rise gently at the corners; glazed tile only on palaces and great temples |
| The Forbidden City look | Ming and Qing | The Song palace in Kaifeng: red walls, gold-glazed tiles, broad halls on high stone platforms |

## Architecture by rank

Every building in the game says something about who lives there.

| Rank | Roof | Walls and columns | Details |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Imperial** (palace) | Yellow glazed tiles (yellow is for the emperor only) | Vermilion walls and columns, white marble platforms | Large bracket sets (*dougong*) under the eaves, painted beams |
| **Official** (yamen, prefecture, garrison) | Grey tube tiles, dark ridge with end ornaments | Vermilion columns and gates; red walls round the compound | Studded double gates, a name board over the gate, stone lions |
| **Temple** (Buddhist or Daoist) | Grey or green glazed tiles; pagodas | Red walls; yellow or ochre for Buddhist halls | Incense burner in the court, bell and drum towers, banners |
| **Wealthy commoner** (merchant, landlord, manor) | Grey tube tiles, gentle upturned corners | Whitewashed walls framed in dark or red-brown timber | Courtyard plan, lattice windows, a decorated gate |
| **Town shop or tavern** | Grey tiles or tiles over a lean-to awning | Open shopfronts with removable boards; whitewash | Vertical shop signs, a **wine flag** (a cloth banner reading 酒) on a pole, lanterns, and for big restaurants a festooned gateway (*cailou huanmen*, see the Qingming scroll) |
| **Peasant or fisherman** | Thatch (straw), held down with poles | Rammed earth or wattle-and-daub, ochre and brown | Low fences, drying racks, grain jars, a well |
| **Bandit stronghold** | Thatch or rough tile, palisades of sharpened logs | Log walls, a timber gate tower | Banners (Liangshan's reads 替天行道, "Act on Heaven's Behalf"), weapon racks, a meeting hall |

**Song features to keep in mind:**

- Roofs are rows of **round tube tiles** (筒瓦) running **down the slope**, with round tile-ends (瓦當) along the eave.
- A gentle upward sweep at the eave corners (起翹). The main ridge carries a curled ornament at each end (鴟吻).
- **Bracket sets** (斗拱) sit on the columns under the eave: big on halls, small on houses.
- The commonest window is the **vertical-slat window** (直欞窗). Ornate lattices belong to rich houses.
- Buildings stand on a **stone or rammed-earth platform**, with steps up to the door.
- City walls are rammed earth faced with brick, with crenellations and a gate tower over each gate.

## Clothing

| Who | Head | Body | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Officials and clerks (Song Jiang as clerk, Gao Qiu) | *Futou* (幞頭) cap, black; officials' versions have long stiff side "wings" | Round-collared robe (圓領袍) to the ankles, belt with plaques | Robe colours by rank: purple and red high, green and blue lower |
| Soldiers and constables | Helmet, or a cloth wrap with a red tassel | Lamellar or leather armour over a short robe; red or black uniforms | Spear, sabre, crossbow |
| Heroes and wanderers (Wu Song, Lin Chong on the run) | Topknot with cloth band, or a felt hat in winter | Short jacket over trousers, leg wrappings, straw sandals or boots | Pilgrim Wu Song: monk's iron hoop on loose hair, prayer beads |
| Labourers, boatmen, fishermen | Bare topknot or a straw hat | Short hemp jacket, often open; rolled trousers | Barefoot on boats; carrying poles |
| Women (Pan Jinlian, Hu Sanniang, Sun Erniang) | High bun with hairpins | Long open-front jacket (褙子, *beizi*) over a long skirt; narrow silhouette | Hu Sanniang in light armour with twin sabres; Sun Erniang an innkeeper with bangles |
| Monks (Lu Zhishen, the Wutai monks) | Shaved head (Lu Zhishen keeps a bristly beard) | Grey or brown robe with a patched kasaya over one shoulder | Meditation beads, iron staff, monk's spade |
| Daoists (Gongsun Sheng, Luo Zhenren) | Topknot in a small crown with a hairpin | Wide-sleeved robe, often blue or yellow | Horsetail whisk, sword, gourd |
| The emperor (Huizong) | Formal crown or a *futou* with straight wings | Yellow round-collared robe | A painter and calligrapher: refined, not martial |

## Places, and the look of each

These match the location themes in the Music Room (`tools/music-room.html`), so picture and music can agree.

- **The Eastern Capital (Kaifeng).** Dense two-storey shops, restaurant gateways hung with silk, a great arched timber
  bridge over the Bian canal, camel trains, crowds. Rich and loud.
- **County towns** (Yanggu, Yuncheng): one long main street, single-storey tiled shops, a yamen with red gates, a teahouse.
- **River ports** (Jiangzhou, Jieyang): stilted houses, jetties, sampans and grain boats, willows, a famous tower
  (the Xunyang Tower is a three-storey restaurant hall on the riverbank).
- **The north** (Daming, Yanmen, Cangzhou): thick rammed-earth walls, flat-roofed garrison buildings, snow, poplars, camps.
- **The south** (Hangzhou): white walls with black-tile copings, arched stone bridges, gardens, West Lake, pagodas on the hills.
- **Villages:** thatched farmhouses, threshing floors, mulberry and willow, a manor house among them.
- **Buddhist temples** (Mount Wutai, the Xiangguo Monastery, Liuhe Pagoda): red walls, axial courtyards, Guardian King
  statues at the gate, a bell tower and a drum tower, pines.
- **Daoist mountains** (Dragon-Tiger Mountain, Erxian): halls on terraces up the slope, mist, a hermit's thatched hut, cranes.
- **Ruins** (Waguan Monastery): collapsed roofs, weeds through the paving, toppled statues, cold light.
- **Bandit mountains** (Liangshan, Erlong, Qingfeng): palisades and gate towers on a pass, rough halls, banners, weapon racks;
  Liangshan's Hall of Loyalty (忠義堂) is a big timber hall with the 替天行道 banner before it.
- **Inns and taverns:** a wine flag on a pole, benches under a big tree, a kitchen at the back (and at Cross Slope,
  something terrible in it).

## Game formats

| Asset | Size | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Map tile | 16 × 16 px | Seen from above, three-quarter view (fronts of walls visible). The game renders at 320 × 240 and scales up 3×. |
| Field sprite sheet | 3 columns × 4 rows; 24 × 32 px per cell (72 × 128) | Columns: stand, step, step. Rows: facing down, left, right, up. Feet at the bottom edge of the cell. File: `assets/sprites/<id>.png`. |
| Portrait | Any size, about 6:7 | Shown in a 144 × 168 frame, top-aligned. File: `assets/portraits/<id>.png`. |
| Expression sheet | 3 columns × 2 rows | Order: normal, angry, sad, smile, shock, concerned. File: `assets/portraits/<id>-expressions.png`. |

List new art in `assets/manifest.js`. Character ids are in `js/data/chars.js` and in `WM.npc(...)` calls.

## The map tiles, one by one

Each letter is how the tile is written in the map files. Use it when you replace a tile. To see how every tile is drawn now, in a small scene of its own, open `tools/tile-atlas.html` while the game is served (for example <http://localhost:8766/tools/tile-atlas.html>).

| Code | What it is | Song pointers |
|---|---|---|
| `^` | Grey tiled roof | Tube tiles down the slope; dark ridge with curled end ornaments; tile-ends along the eave; corners rising gently |
| `Z` | Gold glazed roof | Imperial yellow glaze; only the palace and the emperor's temples |
| `v` | Red-brown roof | Temples and great halls (could also be green glaze for Daoist halls) |
| `z` | Thatch | Straw held down by poles or ropes; peasants, fishermen, bandit huts |
| `W` | House wall | Whitewash between dark timber columns, a vermilion beam on small brackets under the eave, a stone base |
| `O` | House wall with window | Vertical-slat window, paper behind |
| `R` | Red wall | Vermilion compound wall of a temple or office, capped with grey tiles |
| `#` | Stone wall | City or fortress wall: brick-faced rammed earth with crenellations |
| `G` | City gate | Deep arched or square passage under a gate tower, heavy studded doors |
| `D` | Door | Double leaves with rows of studs and ring knockers; a name plaque above |
| `=` | Paving | Rectangular stone slabs; courtyards and official streets |
| `:` | Dirt road | Packed earth with cart ruts |
| `L` | Lantern post | Red paper lantern on a wooden post |
| `I` | Signboard | A tall notice board under a little tiled roof; for taverns, ideally a cloth wine flag reading 酒 on a pole (a separate tile would be worth adding) |
| `Y` | Banner | Liangshan's apricot-yellow 替天行道 banner, red border |
| `F` | Fence | Bamboo or wattle for villages; a palisade of logs for strongholds |
| `p` | Well | Stone curb, wooden windlass |
| `j` | Jar | Big glazed storage jar (wine, grain, pickles) |
| `t`, `c`, `h`, `k` | Table, counter, shelf, bed | Square tables with benches (people sat on chairs and benches by Song times); a curtained bed; shelves of scrolls and jars |
| `A` | Altar | Long red table with incense burner and candlesticks |
| `U` | Statue | Buddha, a Guardian King, or a deified hero |
| `Q` | Stone tablet | Stele on a stone tortoise |
| `B` | Bridge | Timber arch (Kaifeng's Rainbow Bridge) or stone slab |
| `o` | Boat | Sampan with a woven awning |
| `T`, `P`, `H`, `b` | Tree, pine, poplar, bamboo | Willows by water, pines on mountains, poplars in the north, bamboo in the south |

## A prompt to start from

> Pixel art, 16×16 top-down three-quarter-view RPG tileset in the style of Suikoden II. Northern Song dynasty China,
> about 1110 CE, after the painting *Along the River During the Qingming Festival*. Grey tube-tile roofs running down
> the slope with gently upturned corners and curled ridge ornaments, whitewashed walls between dark timber columns,
> vermilion beams on small brackets under the eaves, vertical-slat windows, studded double doors, stone platforms.
> Muted natural palette. No Japanese or Qing-dynasty elements.

> Pixel art RPG character sprite sheet, 3 frames × 4 directions, 24×32 per frame, Northern Song dynasty China.
> [Character]: [clothing from the table above]. Hair in a topknot under a black *futou* cap (no braid, no shaved forehead).
> Clean dark outline, limited palette, in the style of Suikoden II.
